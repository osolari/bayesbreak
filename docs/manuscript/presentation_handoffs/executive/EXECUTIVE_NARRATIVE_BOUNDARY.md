# Executive narrative boundary

The executive route may simplify equations but must preserve the model assumptions, established versus planned status, and the distinction between real execution and valid interpretation.

It may discuss implementation stages, validation criteria, risks, resources, publication, and release decisions because these are already approved in the executive summary.

It may not:

- rename the work or redirect it away from generalized hierarchical Bayesian segmentation;
- replace statistical terms with branding;
- imply that all exponential-family or nonconjugate models are exact without the stated conjugacy and integrability conditions;
- present the finite latent-group criterion as a normalized mixture likelihood;
- represent the two excluded historical calculations as scientific results;
- omit the remaining EP logistic-normal timeout or erase the historical 17-unresolved verification state when discussing implementation readiness;
- introduce claims, results, dates, budgets, or resource estimates not supported by the source artifacts;
- introduce a roadmap not already present in the executive summary and listed in the executive brief.
