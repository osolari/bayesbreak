\section{Limitations, failure modes, and counterexamples}
\label{sec:limitations}

The preceding sections establish the BayesBreak framework as a modular exact-Bayesian
segmentation pipeline with explicit theoretical guarantees on conjugate-block inference
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ef-integral}, Proposition~\ref{prop:bb-complexity}), posterior-odds
stability under non-conjugate approximation (Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-block-error},
Proposition~\ref{prop:stability}, Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-bounds}), latent-group
identifiability up to label permutation (Proposition~\ref{prop:latent-identifiability},
Example~\ref{ex:label-switch-counterexample}), and joint MAP correctness
(Theorem~\ref{thm:map-correctness}). This section collects the corresponding limitations
and named failure modes, so that downstream users can reason about applicability
before running the framework on a new problem class.

\paragraph{Computational regime.}
The DP layer is $\Theta(k_{\max} n^2)$ time and at least $\Theta(k_{\max} n)$ memory
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bb-complexity}). The framework is therefore appropriate for series
lengths $n$ in the thousands and modest segment ceilings $k_{\max}$; it is \emph{not} an
online detector and is not competitive with linear-time penalized methods (e.g.\ PELT) at
$n\gtrsim 10^5$ unless block precomputation is dramatically sparsified. The DP itself
cannot be replaced with a coarser approximation without losing the exact posterior
guarantees that motivate the framework; the right substitution for very large $n$ is a
sliding-window decomposition (planned, not implemented here). Any such decomposition would be an approximation to the posterior over the full sequence: it may be useful as an engineering device, but it would no longer inherit Theorem~\ref{thm:dp-correctness} or the exact boundary-marginal identities without an additional approximation theorem.

\paragraph{Block-model misspecification.}
All guarantees are conditional on the chosen exponential-family block model. If the true
within-segment distribution is not in the chosen family (e.g.\ heavy-tailed observations
under a Gaussian block, or zero-inflation under a Poisson block), block evidences become
biased and the posterior over $k$ inherits that bias. The framework provides
posterior-predictive diagnostics for this case (Section~\ref{sec:families-predictive}),
but does \emph{not} adapt the block family automatically. A practical recipe is to
compare the implemented block families on the same data (Table~\ref{tab:single_quant})
and reserve the chosen specification for the body of the paper.

\paragraph{Non-conjugate approximation regime.}
The posterior-odds bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:stability} is linear in $k_{\max}$, so
non-conjugate analyses with $k_{\max}\gg 30$ must verify a correspondingly smaller $\varepsilon$
to keep $(k+k')\varepsilon$ small. Routine-by-routine $\varepsilon$ rates are in
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-bounds}; the EP row of Table~\ref{tab:nonconj_tradeoff} is the
canonical failure mode (EP need not satisfy a uniform $\varepsilon$ when its iteration fails to
converge).

\paragraph{Latent-group identifiability.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:latent-identifiability} identifies the latent-group template
mixture up to permutation of the group labels;
Example~\ref{ex:label-switch-counterexample} makes the indeterminacy explicit at the
two-group, two-subject scale. Any label-level reporting therefore requires a
deterministic anchoring convention (e.g.\ ordering templates by $k_g$ or by lexicographic
order on $t^{(g)}$). The EM algorithm of Section~\ref{sec:latent-em} is not guaranteed to
recover the global maximizer of the finite template-mixture objective; the
recommendation of $R\ge 5$ restarts in Section~\ref{sec:latent-em} is a defense against
this but is not a guarantee.

\paragraph{Boundary semantics.}
Boundary-event marginals $p(b_i=1\mid y,k)$ and the joint MAP segmentation
$\widehat t^{\mathrm{MAP}}(k)$ are different posterior summaries (Remark~\ref{rem:marg-vs-joint})
and must not be conflated. Report both whenever boundary semantics matter for the downstream task.

\paragraph{Partition-prior sensitivity.}
The posterior over segment counts $p(k\mid y)$ is mildly sensitive to the prior $p(k)$
and substantially sensitive to the length factor $g(\Delta_x(i,j))$ when the design grid is
irregular. The framework supplies design-aware partition priors
(Section~\ref{sec:irregular}); the well-log results table (Table~\ref{tab:real_welllog};
length-aware row reserved) is the natural place to quantify this sensitivity for users.
Until that ablation row is populated, users are advised to compare an index-uniform prior to
at least one length-aware alternative and report any disagreement explicitly.

\paragraph{Assumption-to-failure-mode map.}
Assumption~\ref{ass:standing-offline} fails when the candidate boundary grid omits scientifically meaningful changepoints or when segment independence is inappropriate. Assumption~\ref{ass:factorizing-prior} fails when boundary dependence cannot be represented by a product cohesion, for example when enforcing global spacing patterns that depend on more than adjacent segment lengths. Assumption~\ref{ass:cond-indep-subjects} fails when shared-boundary replicates have unmodeled subject correlations after conditioning on segment parameters. Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-block-error} fails when non-conjugate block routines cannot certify a blockwise log-evidence tolerance. Assumption~\ref{ass:prediction-independence} fails when prediction units are dependent after conditioning on the exported group model. These failures are model violations, not merely numerical warnings; the recommended response is to change the block model, prior class, dependence structure, or prediction target before interpreting posterior probabilities.

\paragraph{Out-of-scope settings.}
The framework does \emph{not} address: (i) streaming or online detection (use
\citet{adamsmackay2007bocpd}); (ii) within-segment trends or change-in-slope models (use
piecewise-polynomial regression, e.g.\ \citet{ruggieri2014bcpvs}); (iii) variable-dimensional
posterior simulation over $k$ with explicit posterior samples of $t$
(use~\citet{green1995rjMCMC,fearnhead2006exact}); (iv) network or spatial segmentation on
non-totally-ordered designs. The motivating reason to use BayesBreak is exact
sum-product inference under \emph{ordered} designs and \emph{piecewise-constant} block
means; the moment a problem falls outside that scope, a different framework is more
appropriate.

\paragraph{Identifiability failures (named).}
Three named identifiability failure modes are documented in the body and bear repeating here.
(i) \emph{Label switching} in the latent-template mixture: under a saturated specification
$G=G^\star$ the mixture is identifiable up to label permutation
(Proposition~\ref{prop:latent-identifiability}; \citet{teicher1963identifiability}); label
switching is a posterior-summary, not a posterior-density, ambiguity. (ii)
\emph{Overspecified-$G$ redundancy}: at $G>G^\star$, two distinct $(\pi,\tau)$ configurations
can yield identical mixture densities (Remark~\ref{rem:teicher-overspec}); EM remains monotone
but no longer returns a unique solution under this redundancy. Holdout-predictive selection of
$G$ (Definition~\ref{def:metric-loglik}) is the recommended mitigation. (iii)
\emph{Shared-boundary degeneracy}: when two candidate boundary vectors differ only on blocks of
length zero or on blocks unobserved under every subject, the shared-boundary identifiability
result (Proposition~\ref{prop:shared-boundary-identifiability}) fails by design; the support
condition of Definition~\ref{def:admissible-blocks} rules out such configurations from the DP.

\paragraph{Non-conjugate approximation outside the small-$\varepsilon$ regime.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:stability} provides a uniform stability bound on posterior odds when the
non-conjugate block routine satisfies Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-block-error} with a small
log-evidence tolerance $\varepsilon$. The EP row of Table~\ref{tab:nonconj_tradeoff} exhibits
$\max|\Delta\log A^{(0)}|\approx 14.5$ on the worst reachable block, well outside the
small-$\varepsilon$ regime; the boundary F1 reported in that row is therefore a descriptive
diagnostic and not a confirmation of the stability bound. The recommended response when the
empirical $\varepsilon$ is large is (a) to verify the block-approximation diagnostic for every
non-conjugate routine before reading the DP-aggregated posterior at face value, and (b) to fall
back to quadrature or to a tighter PG mean-field routine where the per-block log-evidence error
is verifiably small.

\paragraph{Decision flowchart: which branch to use.}
The choice among the BayesBreak branches is governed by the following short rule of thumb,
stated as a checklist rather than as a numerical decision rule.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=2.3em]
\item \emph{Single sequence, conjugate likelihood.} Use the exact conjugate DP of
Section~\ref{sec:dp} with Section~\ref{sec:families-summary}'s closed-form block evidences.
This is the default and the only branch whose posterior is fully exact at machine precision.
\item \emph{Single sequence, non-conjugate likelihood.} Use one of
$\{f_{\mathrm{Lap}},f_{\mathrm{JJ}},f_{\mathrm{EP}},f_{\mathrm{PG}},f_{\mathrm{Q}}\}$ on each
block; verify $\max|\log\widehat A^{(0)}_{ij}-\log A^{(0)}_{ij}|\le\varepsilon$ via the
diagnostic of Table~\ref{tab:nonconj_tradeoff} before reading the DP output. Fall back to
quadrature when the diagnostic is not satisfied.
\item \emph{Multi-subject data with shared boundaries.} Use the pooled DP of
Theorem~\ref{thm:multisubject} and Algorithm~\ref{alg:pool-shared-boundary} when subjects share
a common index grid; shift to known-groups or latent-template when subjects are heterogeneous in
their boundary structure.
\item \emph{Heterogeneous boundary structure across subjects.} Use known-groups pooling when
group labels are observed (Section~\ref{sec:groups-known}); use latent-template EM
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:latent-em-detail}) when labels are unobserved, with $G$ selected by
held-out predictive log-likelihood (Definition~\ref{def:metric-loglik}) and identifiability
verified per the preceding paragraph.
\item \emph{Prediction on new data.} Use the prediction layer of Section~\ref{sec:prediction}
after segmentation. Prediction-input class (Definition~\ref{def:prediction-cases}) determines
whether the per-unit posterior-predictive or the resegmentation routine is appropriate; both
share the same block-evidence interface as inference.
\end{enumerate}
Failure of any required hypothesis is a signal to fall back one branch level or to change the
block model, not to interpret the posterior at face value.

\paragraph{Scope of the empirical evaluation in this paper.}
The synthetic suite and the four real-data figures are the implemented demonstrations of the
framework; conclusions in this paper are restricted to claims that the implemented framework
recovers what it was designed to recover. Broader competitiveness claims require the planned
external comparators (PELT, wild binary segmentation, SMUCE, RJMCMC, Fearnhead's exact DP) and
the partition-prior / pooling / likelihood ablations populated by the finalized benchmarking
pipeline.
