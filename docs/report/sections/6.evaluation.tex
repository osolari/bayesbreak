\section{Experiments and results}
\label{sec:experiments}

We report a focused set of synthetic experiments that validate the core BayesBreak inference (posterior over the number and locations of boundaries) and exercise the main extensions implemented in the repository (likelihood families, latent-group pooling, and non-conjugate approximations). All figures and tables in this section are generated by the companion scripts in \texttt{scripts/figures/} and \texttt{scripts/tables/} and written to the \texttt{results/} directory.

\subsection{Single-sequence Gaussian illustration}
\label{sec:single_synth}

We begin with a simple Gaussian sequence with two changepoints. Figure~\ref{fig:single_synth} shows (i) the marginal posterior probability of a boundary after each time index and (ii) the selected piecewise-constant posterior mean curve together with pointwise credible intervals for the segment means (conditional on the selected segmentation). Boundary posterior mass concentrates at the two ground-truth changepoints, and uncertainty in the segment means is larger in shorter segments (as expected under a conjugate Normal prior).

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig1_synthetic_gaussian.png}
    \caption{Single-sequence Gaussian example. Top: marginal posterior boundary probabilities $p(b_i=1\mid y)$ for each interior index $i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}$. Bottom: noisy observations (points), true latent mean (dashed), and the selected piecewise-constant posterior mean fit; shaded bands indicate 90\% credible intervals for the segment means conditional on the selected segmentation.}
    \label{fig:single_synth}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Notes on scope.}
The experiments above are synthetic by design: they provide controlled settings where ground truth is available and allow unit-style validation of posterior quantities (boundary posteriors, calibration, and approximation error). Applying the full model suite to large real-world benchmarks and providing extensive ablations (e.g., prior sensitivity, $p(k)$ choices, and robustness to mis-specification) are left as future work.
