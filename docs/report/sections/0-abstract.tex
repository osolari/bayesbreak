\abstract{
Bayesian change-point and segmentation models provide uncertainty-aware piecewise-constant representations of ordered data, but exact inference is often tied to narrow likelihood classes, single-sequence settings, or index-uniform designs. We present \texttt{BayesBreak}\footnote{Reference implementation: \url{https://github.com/osolari/bayesbreak}.}, a modular offline Bayesian segmentation framework built around a separation between local block modeling and global partition inference. Each candidate block contributes a marginal likelihood and any required moment numerators; a global dynamic program combines those block scores into posterior quantities over segment counts, boundary locations, and latent signals.

For weighted exponential-family likelihoods with conjugate priors and a segment-factorized partition prior, block evidences and posterior moments are available in closed form from cumulative sufficient statistics. This yields exact sum-product inference for $p(y\mid k)$, $p(k\mid y)$, boundary marginals, and Bayes regression curves. We distinguish these posterior summaries from the \emph{joint} MAP segmentation, which is recovered by a separate max-sum backtracking recursion.

The framework extends to design-aware partition priors for irregularly spaced observations, exact boundary-posterior pooling across replicates with shared boundaries, and a latent-template mixture for unknown group membership with exact coordinate updates for the stated finite template-mixture objective. For non-conjugate GLM blocks, BayesBreak reuses the same DP layer with deterministic local approximations (Laplace, variational Jaakkola--Jordan and P\'olya--Gamma mean-field, expectation propagation, or one-dimensional quadrature). Under a uniform per-block log-evidence error $\varepsilon$, the induced perturbation of posterior odds is bounded by $(k+k')\varepsilon$ for $k$-odds and by $2k\varepsilon$ for fixed-$k$ boundary odds.

At prediction time, BayesBreak defines posterior-predictive scoring interfaces for new sequences and set-valued units under exported segmentations or Bayes curves. The empirical section separates completed diagnostics from future benchmarking: it contains a completed synthetic recovery/calibration/scaling suite and four completed real-data case-study fits--well-log NMR geology, array-CGH copy number, equity-return volatility, and CpG-atlas methylation--while companion quantitative comparator and ablation tables are planned outputs of the benchmarking pipeline. Empirically, on the bundled synthetics: boundary marginals are well calibrated ($\mathrm{ECE}\approx 0.010$, Brier $\approx 0.011$); the latent-template mixture recovers two groups with different changepoint patterns at $96\%$ hard accuracy under moderate noise ($\sigma=1.0$, $n_{\mathrm{seq}}=24$); the posterior over the segment count sharpens at low noise and drifts to larger $\hat k$ under high noise, illustrating principled model-selection uncertainty rather than a single point estimate; and the exact-DP runtime grows nearly linearly in $n$ over $n\in[50,800]$ (empirical log-log slope $\approx 1.07$ at $k_{\max}=10$ and $\approx 1.14$ at $k_{\max}=20$), well inside the $\mathcal{O}(k_{\max}n^2)$ worst case thanks to cumulative-sufficient-statistics block caching.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Bayesian segmentation; change-point detection; dynamic programming; product partitions; hierarchical models.
}
