\section*{Scope and inferential objectives}
This book develops \emph{Generalized Hierarchical Bayesian Segmentation with Irregular Designs,
Multi-Sequence Hierarchies, and Grouped/Latent-Group Designs}. Its basic objects are segmentwise
integrated likelihoods, proper priors over ordered partitions, posterior distributions over segment
counts and changepoints, Bayes regression curves, and joint MAP partitions. The mathematical
constructions build on established product-partition models and exact offline Bayesian changepoint
inference. Extensions cover physical-coordinate priors, shared-boundary pooling, finite-template
clustering, and numerical integration of nonconjugate segment models.

\section*{Exact and approximate calculations}
Analytic segment integration combined with sum-product dynamic programming yields exact finite
posterior quantities under the specified model. Max-sum backtracking yields a joint MAP partition,
not an independently chosen collection of marginal boundary modes. Where segment integrals require
Laplace approximation, variational approximations, expectation propagation, or quadrature, the
global recursion is exact only for the resulting approximate segment scores. No unverified finite
error certificate is implied by convergence of a local approximation.

\section*{Empirical interpretation}
The reported figures and tables are computations under their stated observation laws, data
transformations, priors, and sample units. Results from different likelihood families or different
observation supports are not directly comparable marginal likelihoods. Agreement with an estimated
segmentation is distinct from independent boundary accuracy. The current array-CGH precision model
requires a corrected likelihood implementation and a new fit before its numerical result can
support a precision-aware sampling claim. More extensive experiments are defined prospectively,
with outputs left unfilled until actually executed and checked.

\section*{Organization}
Chapters~\ref{ch:orientation}--\ref{ch:map} present the formulation, conjugate block integration
and partition recursions. Chapters~\ref{ch:priors}--\ref{ch:nonconj} develop irregular designs,
shared boundaries, finite templates and local numerical approximations. Chapters~\ref{ch:prediction}--
\ref{ch:realdata} cover prediction, algorithms, verification and examples. The concluding chapters
state failure conditions and open experimental requirements. The appendices provide annotated
literature, numerical implementation principles, and prospective experimental designs.
