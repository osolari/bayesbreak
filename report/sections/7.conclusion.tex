\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

BayesBreak is organized around a simple but powerful decomposition: local block integrals capture
all model-specific work, while a global dynamic program handles posterior inference over contiguous
partitions. That decomposition yields exact offline Bayesian segmentation whenever block evidences
are available in closed form, and it extends directly to controlled deterministic approximations
when closed-form block integration is unavailable. Within that framework we obtained exact posterior
quantities for segment counts, boundary marginals, and Bayes regression curves; clarified the
necessary distinction between those posterior summaries and an exported joint MAP segmentation;
showed how irregular designs are most naturally handled through design-aware partition priors;
and preserved exactness for pooled shared-boundary analyses across replicates.

For unknown groups, we introduced a latent-template mixture with an explicit finite objective.
EM updates are monotone for that objective in the generalized-EM sense of
\citet{dempster1977em}: responsibilities are exact for the current templates, each template
update is an exact responsibility-weighted max-sum DP, and the finite-mixture objective is
non-decreasing across iterations (Theorem~\ref{thm:em-monotone}). For non-conjugate blocks, the
DP layer is unchanged, and approximation guarantees are stated in terms of block-evidence error
and induced posterior-odds stability (Proposition~\ref{prop:stability},
Corollary~\ref{cor:ranking}).

\paragraph{What is exact and what is approximate.}
For clarity in downstream use, the manuscript draws a sharp line between three regimes.
\emph{Exact at machine precision:} the sum-product DP on a conjugate exponential-family block
family with constant-time block evaluation (Sections~\ref{sec:ef}--\ref{sec:dp}), the
forward--backward duality identity (Proposition~\ref{prop:fb-duality}), the pooled
shared-boundary DP for conditionally independent subjects
(Theorem~\ref{thm:multisubject}; Algorithm~\ref{alg:pool-shared-boundary}), and the max-sum
backtracking that produces $\widehat t^{\mathrm{MAP}}(k)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:map-correctness}).
\emph{Exact for a finite-template objective but not Bayesian-exact:} the latent-template EM of
Section~\ref{sec:latent-em} and Algorithm~\ref{alg:latent-em-detail}; EM optimizes a
finite-mixture objective that is identifiable up to relabeling under
\citet{teicher1963identifiability} at $G=G^\star$ but is non-unique at $G>G^\star$
(Remark~\ref{rem:teicher-overspec}). \emph{Approximate at the block level, exact at the DP
layer:} the non-conjugate routines (Laplace, JJ, EP, PG mean field, 1D quadrature) with
posterior-odds stability quantified by $\varepsilon$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:stability}; large empirical $\varepsilon$ (as in the EP row of
Table~\ref{tab:nonconj_tradeoff}) places the run outside the small-$\varepsilon$ regime and
requires the diagnostic-based fallback of the limitations section.

Empirically, the archived project provides a coherent synthetic validation suite showing posterior
boundary recovery, family portability, calibration behavior, latent-group separation, local
approximation trade-offs, and runtime scaling (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). That suite is
accompanied by four real-data case studies with completed BayesBreak fits (well-log NMR geology, array-CGH
copy number, S\&P 500 volatility regimes, and CpG-atlas methylation; see
Sections~\ref{sec:realdata-welllog}--\ref{sec:realdata-methylation} and reproduction pipelines in
Appendix~\ref{app:real-data}), each chosen to exercise a different part of the framework. The
companion per-case-study quantitative tables are now partially populated from the same archived
fits as the figures: each table reports the cells that come directly from the recorded JSON
sidecar of its figure, and reserves rows or columns that require an external annotation load,
a comparator refit, or a second pipeline run.

\paragraph{Deferred empirical work.}
Four empirical extensions are explicitly deferred to the next-pass benchmarking run: (i)
head-to-head comparison against the frequentist baselines PELT, wild binary segmentation, and
SMUCE on the four real-data case studies under a fixed boundary-tolerance protocol; (ii)
head-to-head comparison against the Bayesian baselines RJMCMC of \citet{green1995rjMCMC} and
Fearnhead's exact DP \citep{fearnhead2006exact} on the same case studies; (iii) partition-prior
and length-factor ablations populating the second row of Tables~\ref{tab:real_welllog} and
\ref{tab:real_spx} and the held-out-region row of Table~\ref{tab:real_methylation}; and (iv) a
scaling study pushing $n$ into the tens of thousands to exercise the memory--time trade-offs of
Remark~\ref{rem:mem-time}. None of these extensions requires modifying the BayesBreak inferential
core; each only requires the corresponding block-evidence or pipeline harness.

\paragraph{Downstream applications.}
The framework was designed with downstream genomic and biomedical applications in mind, and the
real-data case studies map cleanly onto them. The Coriell array-CGH fit
(Section~\ref{sec:realdata-cgh}) is the natural template for copy-number-alteration calling on
ultra-low-pass whole-genome sequencing or ctDNA, where the shared-boundary multi-subject DP
contracts subject heterogeneity into a single segmentation while preserving subject-level
parameter posteriors. The CpG-atlas methylation fit
(Section~\ref{sec:realdata-methylation}) is the natural template for segmenting methylation
$\beta$-profiles in cell-type-of-origin and tissue-of-origin deconvolution pipelines, where the
Beta-response non-conjugate block exercises the quadrature branch under known per-CpG precision.
The S\&P 500 volatility-regime fit (Section~\ref{sec:realdata-spx}) demonstrates how a single
non-genomic time-series application uses the same machinery without any framework change.
