\abstract{%
BayesBreak is a generalized hierarchical Bayesian method for offline multiple-changepoint segmentation of ordered data. For a regular exponential-family observation model with a proper segmentwise conjugate prior and finite normalizing constants, cumulative sufficient statistics determine the marginal likelihood and posterior moments of each candidate segment. Dynamic-programming recursions then compute the posterior distribution over the number and locations of changepoints, while a separate max-sum recursion with backtracking returns the joint maximum-a-posteriori partition. The partition calculation is therefore common across supported observation families once their segment marginal likelihoods are supplied.

The method extends this construction to irregular design points, multiple conditionally independent sequences with common changepoints, known groups, and a finite latent-group segmentation criterion. Executed studies include Gaussian, Poisson, Binomial, and Beta-valued examples; a repeated Gaussian boundary-calibration study with ECE approximately 0.010 and Brier score approximately 0.011; a two-group latent-allocation study with 96\% hard assignment accuracy for its stated design; a finite-range runtime study from 0.0455 seconds at $n=50$ to 1.0585 seconds at $n=800$ for $k_{\max}=20$; and four descriptive real-data applications. These are real executed results, but the application studies lack independent reference changepoints and therefore do not establish external accuracy.

The mathematical core and principal hierarchical extensions are established under explicit assumptions. Remaining work concerns implementation corrections, completion of unresolved tests, controlled recomputation of two excluded historical calculations, broader repeated validation, and final release engineering. This document summarizes the scientific position, current evidence, implementation sequence, resource requirements, and decision criteria without introducing claims beyond the technical book and journal paper.}
